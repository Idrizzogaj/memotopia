# Brain-training categories: market research for Memotopia

Research date: 2026-10-07. Based on web search summaries and public pages; app details change often, so re-check before quoting anything. Not legal advice.

## 1. Competitor overview

### Lumosity (Lumos Labs)
- **Categories (5):** Speed, Memory, Attention, Flexibility, Problem Solving.
  - Speed = how fast you perceive and react. Memory = holding and recalling information. Attention = filtering distraction and staying on target. Flexibility = switching between rules or tasks. Problem Solving = planning, logic and working out solutions.
- **Mechanics:** quick reaction games (tap the right target fast), remembering positions or sequences, "ignore the distractors" tasks, rule-switching tasks, puzzle and planning games. 75+ games.
- **Marketing/structure:** onboarding "Fitness Test" sets a baseline; personalised daily workout of a few short games; Brain Performance Index (BPI) per category plus overall, ranked against other users of similar age; progress charts and streaks. Tone: friendly, science-flavoured ("built with university researchers"). After the FTC order the claims got much more modest.
- **Monetisation:** freemium (a few free games/day), annual or monthly subscription.

### Elevate (Elevate Labs)
- **Categories (4 headline):** Writing, Speaking, Reading, Math. Training also described as focus, memory, processing speed, comprehension, precision.
  - Positions itself as "skills for everyday communication and numeracy" rather than "brain".
- **Mechanics:** 40+ short games, e.g. fixing wording and grammar, concise-phrasing exercises, listening-and-recall of spoken passages, mental-arithmetic speed drills, quick vocabulary or word-matching.
- **Marketing/structure:** personalised daily workout (a few games chosen by a model), skill levels per category, a streak calendar, performance stats and "Elevate Score"-style rankings; no big medical claims, tone is productivity and self-improvement. Premium only: more games and the full workout.
- **Monetisation:** freemium, subscription (free tier has a limited daily set).

### Peak
- **Categories (9):** Memory, Attention, Math, Problem Solving, Mental Agility, Language, Coordination, Creativity, Emotion Control.
- **Mechanics:** ~45 games: matching and recall of items, focus-under-distraction tasks, quick arithmetic, puzzles, word games, timing/coordination games.
- **Marketing/structure:** "10 minutes a day" workouts, an AI "Coach" that tracks progress and recommends, "Brain Map" style profile, claims partnership with university academics (Cambridge, NYU), 12M+ downloads. Tone: playful, premium-looking.
- **Monetisation:** freemium plus Pro subscription.

### BrainHQ (Posit Science)
- **Categories (6):** Attention, Brain Speed, Memory, People Skills, Intelligence, Navigation.
  - Brain Speed = processing sensory input quickly and accurately (visual and auditory). People Skills = remembering and reading faces, voices, names. Intelligence = using information, reasoning. Navigation = spatial orientation.
- **Mechanics (named exercise examples):** divided attention, fast two-target decisions, tracking moving targets, visual/auditory sweep discrimination, spotting fine differences. 25+ exercises.
- **Marketing/structure:** the most science-forward brand: cites published clinical studies, targets older adults and clinicians; adaptive difficulty, a "daily workout", progress per exercise and category, a "Brain Training" star/level system. Tone: clinical, serious.
- **Monetisation:** limited free daily access, subscription, also sold via health plans and libraries.

### CogniFit
- **Categories:** memory, attention, coordination, reasoning, perception, plus processing speed and reaction time; claims up to ~22-30 measured abilities.
- **Mechanics:** 100+ games across those abilities; clinical-style assessments (also sold as research/clinical tools).
- **Marketing/structure:** starts with a cognitive assessment, gives a 0-100 cognitive score by domain, "brain age" comparison, individualised training plan ("Individualized Training System"). Tone: clinical and wellness; this is the style most at risk of drifting into health claims.
- **Monetisation:** freemium trial, subscription (plus professional licences).

### NeuroNation
- **Categories (4 courses):** Memory, Focus/Concentration, Logic, Speed (also working memory and executive functions in medical variants).
- **Mechanics:** 50+ exercises: recall of sequences, find-the-target and filter tasks, logic puzzles, mental calculation and quick decisions.
- **Marketing/structure:** goal-based training courses (e.g. "improve concentration"), levelled progression, personalised plan, German-science positioning, a separate medical-device-style branch for clinical use. Tone: sober, goal-oriented.
- **Monetisation:** freemium, premium subscription.

### Others
- **Impulse:** 100M+ downloads; memory, attention, concentration, maths, problem solving; personalised workout plans, 3-day trial, subscription.
- **Brain Age (Nintendo):** not an app but the cultural origin: simple maths/reading drills and a playful "brain age" number; sold as a game (entertainment framing).
- **Fit Brains:** casual game portal with social leaderboards and achievements.

## 2. Patterns across the market
1. Almost everyone uses 4-9 categories; **Memory, Attention/Focus, Speed, and Problem Solving/Logic** appear nearly everywhere.
2. Common user journey: quick **baseline test -> per-category scores -> daily 3-5 game workout -> streaks and charts**.
3. A single headline number (BPI, cognitive score, "brain age") drives engagement; it works as a game score, not as a medical measure.
4. Personalisation ("your plan", "your weakest area") is the main selling point.
5. Social/competition layers (rankings vs age group) are common; Memotopia's player-vs-player challenges are a real differentiator.
6. Nearly all are freemium subscriptions with a limited free daily set.

## 3. Claims and compliance

### FTC v. Lumos Labs (January 2016)
- FTC alleged Lumosity's ads deceived consumers with unfounded claims that games help users do better at work and school, and reduce or delay age-related cognitive decline and conditions like dementia, Alzheimer's, stroke, TBI and PTSD.
- Also criticised: testimonials gathered through a prize promotion without disclosing it; auto-renewal cancellation was hard.
- Outcome: Lumos Labs paid $2 million (the original judgment was about $50 million, suspended due to ability to pay), had to notify subscribers and offer easy cancellation, and is **barred from making claims about cognitive benefits (performance, age-related decline, health conditions) unless it has competent and reliable scientific evidence** (for health-related claims, human clinical testing).
- Related: Stanford Center on Longevity / Max Planck consensus statement (2014, 70+ scientists) said the literature does not support that brain games improve everyday cognition or prevent decline; counter-statements from industry (e.g. Posit Science/BrainHQ) disagreed. Expect scepticism from reviewers.
- Similar rule everywhere: US FTC Act section 5 (deceptive claims need substantiation), and in the EU/Sweden the Marknadsforingslagen and UCPD (misleading advertising; consumer agency "Konsumentverket" expects claims to be substantiated). Auto-renewal and testimonial disclosure rules also apply to subscriptions.

### Apple App Store
- Guideline 1.4.1 (and related health sections): health/medical apps that could give inaccurate data or be used to diagnose or treat are reviewed with extra scrutiny; accuracy claims must be backed by disclosed methodology; apps may be asked to link sources and tell users to consult a doctor. Wellness-only apps still sometimes get flagged if wording sounds medical.
- Marketing text and screenshots must match what the app does (guideline 2.3, accurate metadata).

### Google Play
- Health Content and Services policy: no misleading health claims that contradict medical consensus or may cause harm; no claims of being as effective as medication; medical-device functionality needs regulatory clearance.
- Store listing must not contain misleading or unsubstantiated claims (Metadata policy).

### Safe wording for Memotopia
**Avoid (do not write):**
- "Improves your IQ / intelligence", "boosts brain power", "makes you smarter".
- "Prevents or delays dementia, Alzheimer's, memory loss", "reverses cognitive decline", "treats ADHD / anxiety".
- "Scientifically proven", "clinically proven", "doctor recommended" without real, cited studies.
- "Brain age", "IQ score", "diagnoses", or any score presented as a medical or clinical measure.
- Undisclosed testimonials or paid reviews; fake counters.

**Prefer:**
- "Fun memory games and challenges", "practice recalling cards, faces, numbers and words".
- "Train your memory technique" (Memotopia's real expertise: memory-sport methods) rather than "train your brain".
- "Track your progress in the games" / "your Memotopia score" (clearly a game score).
- "Games to challenge your focus and attention" (describes the activity, not a benefit).
- Add a short disclaimer: "Memotopia is a game for entertainment and skill practice. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition."
- Make subscription terms, trial length and cancellation clear.

## 4. Suggested category map for Memotopia
Names are generic and descriptive; reuse existing mechanics (card matching, flashing images to recall, placing items in boxes, number/word recall).

| # | Category | Meaning | Simple game ideas (reusing Memotopia mechanics) |
|---|----------|---------|-----------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Memory** | Remember and recall | Pairs (existing); Flash recall of images; Recall a sequence of numbers or words |
| 2 | **Focus** | Stay on target while things distract | Pairs with distractor cards flickering; recall only items of one colour/type from a flashing mix; "spot the one that never repeats" in a stream of images |
| 3 | **Attention** | Notice details and changes | Spot what changed between two flashes; find the odd card out in a grid; count how many times a target appeared in a quick flash |
| 4 | **Speed** | Quick, accurate responses | Timed Pairs (beat the clock); rapid yes/no "seen this before?" on flashing images; quick number comparison |
| 5 | **Visual & Spatial** | Locations and layouts | Boxes (existing): place items back in the right boxes; recall positions on a grid; mirror a flashed pattern |
| 6 | **Numbers** | Digits and mental maths | Number recall (digits in order); remember a few numbers then answer a sum on them; boxes holding numbers, recall which box has the highest |
| 7 | **Words & Language** | Words, names, associations | Word-list recall; match word with image; recall names attached to faces/cards |
| 8 | **Logic & Flexibility** | Rules and switching | Pairs where the matching rule switches (colour then shape); sort items into boxes by a rule that changes mid-round; "what comes next" pattern with cards |

Product ideas borrowed from the market that fit Memotopia:
- A short **baseline test** (one mini-game per category) giving a profile ("your strongest area: Visual & Spatial").
- A **daily workout** of 3-5 mini-games from varied categories; streak and category progress charts.
- A single clearly-labelled **Memotopia score** per category, used in **player-vs-player challenges** (the differentiator versus most competitors).
- Start with Focus and Attention as new areas; they reuse Flash and Pairs with distractors, so little new tech is needed.

## 5. Do not copy
- Do not use competitors' trademarks or product names: Lumosity, BPI / "Brain Performance Index", Elevate, Peak, BrainHQ, CogniFit, NeuroNation, Brain Age, Impulse, and their exercise names (e.g. "Double Decision", "Freeze Frame", "Target Tracker", "Eye for Detail", "Divided Attention" as a branded name).
- Generic category words (Memory, Attention, Focus, Speed, Logic) are fine to use; do not reuse their exact slogans, marketing copy, score names, mascots, UI layouts or descriptions.
- Invent Memotopia's own names for games and scores; write all copy from scratch.
- Do not claim partnerships with universities or "science-backed" status unless Memotopia truly has them.
