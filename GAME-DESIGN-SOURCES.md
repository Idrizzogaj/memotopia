## 2026-11-23 — Saga Chain/Flash order and automatic presentation (recommendation, not yet approved)

All three research sources below are new compared with the preceding relevant source-log entries and come from independent author teams. No general claim about what is common across games is made.

- Brunmair & Richter (2019), Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Author-hosted accepted manuscript: https://www.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf ; publication record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/ . Across 59 studies, interleaving effects depend on task/material characteristics. Supports considering mixed practice while explicitly rejecting a blanket claim that alternating Chain and Flash improves memory. These studies do not test Memotopia, game enjoyment, retention or the proposed route.
- Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans (2001), Executive control of cognitive processes in task switching. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11518143/ ; publisher full text: https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xhp274763.pdf . Four experiments found switch-time costs that were moderated by rule complexity and task cues. Supports a clear game-name cue alongside the distinct colour before each Saga round. Does not establish a particular map order or transition duration as optimal.
- Takase et al. (2022), Processing time affects sequential memory performance beginning at the level of visual encoding. Original research: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0265719 . Faster presentation/shorter inter-stimulus intervals reduced sequential-memory performance in the tested directional-arrow tasks. Supports treating presentation timing as an independent difficulty variable. Does not identify the optimal timing for Flash pictures or establish equivalence between Flash and Chain levels.

Project-specific facts checked locally: Flash's ScaleAnimation has ShowImage at 0.1667 s, HideImage at 1.95 s and AnimationFinished at 2.5833 s. FlashManager automatically triggers the next random cell; it does not use Chain's NEXT study control. Actual playback includes Animator state transitions, so these event times are not a measured wall-clock guarantee. A uniform 90-second budget divided among ten pictures would allocate nine seconds per presentation step, including any transition/gap. At the other end, preserving the original playback and forcing a 52-second memorization cutoff could truncate the 25-picture sequence.

Design judgement proposed to Idriz: try one 40-stop route alternating Chain 1, Flash 1, Chain 2, Flash 2, etc. Keep the same neuron artwork, distinguish game identity with the requested colour plus its name, and preserve separate game/level progress. This offers an earlier change of presentation and avoids finishing Chain 20 before returning to Flash 1; expected enjoyment and difficulty still need playtesting. Do not claim research proves that this is the best order or improves general memory. Retain Flash's existing automatic presentation speed and show its entire sequence; reuse the approved Saga recall-time table, three hearts and shuffle rules. This would intentionally replace the shared memorization deadline for Flash with its native sequence duration and therefore requires Idriz's agreement. Do not silently apply a cutoff that hides unshown pictures. No gameplay implementation changed in this recommendation turn.
